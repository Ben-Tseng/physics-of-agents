Relationship-Credential Routing Mitigates Unauthenticated External Traffic in LLM-Agent Collectives

Ben TsengBuilds on Physics of Agents (El et al., 2026)Code & data: github.com/Ben-Tseng/physics-of-agents

When an LLM-agent router lets external traffic declare its own relationship to the receiver, adding nominal senders steadily pushes the group toward a wrong majority. Checking a credential bound to the relationship before the model reads the message reduces that effect, and the router never has to judge whether the message is true.

01 · The question

Who is allowed to say "I'm your trusted collaborator"?

Multi-agent debate and role-based systems let LLM agents pool reasoning. They also make agents responsive to social signals: how many peers agree, and what relationship a peer claims. Prior work shows that LLM collectives follow numerical majorities and persuasive peers. In most frameworks, sender and relationship fields are ordinary metadata that whoever builds the message can fill in.

This paper isolates one security boundary. An external actor that is not an enrolled agent sends a false answer and labels itself a positive-relationship peer. Content filters and topology defenses examine what a message says. The question here is whether the message is entitled to the route it requests, a property that can be checked deterministically and outside the LLM.

Research question. How does a growing budget of unauthenticated external senders, each carrying a standardized false message, change incorrect-majority outcomes? How much do credential-aware downgrade and drop policies mitigate the effect across two official LLM deployments?

Honest messages carry an HMAC token (FIPS 198-1) over the edge tuple:

policy · noneTrust the claimed relation

Invalid traffic lands in the positive-relationship inbox. This models systems that pass sender and role fields to the prompt without cryptographic enforcement.

policy · downgradeKeep content, remove privilege

The message stays readable in a relationship-unknown channel. An invalid token means the route is unauthorized. It does not mean the claim is false.

policy · dropClosed ingress

Invalid traffic is discarded before prompt construction. In an open system this would also exclude benign unknown senders. That utility cost is not measured here.

03 · Frozen confirmatory design

Two deployments, 20 complete blocks

DeploymentsQwen3.5-27B (Alibaba Cloud Model Studio, official) · Mistral Small 4 mistral-small-2603 (Mistral API)
Task18 held-out, text-only binary math questions; labels balanced 9 / 9
Collective8 honest agents, signed symmetric graph, 14 undirected edges
Dynamics2 synchronous updates, 3 opinion samples per agent and state, T = 0.7
Budgets0, 1, 2, 4 external senders, each targeting up to 4 agents
Policiesnone · downgrade · drop
Block1 control + 3 budgets × 3 policies = 10 cells × 18 questions = 180 episodes
Sample10 seeds × 2 deployments = 20 blocks, 3,600 episodes

Outcome. The primary outcome is an incorrect terminal majority among the eight honest agents. External senders never vote, so the attacker cannot win by adding its own ballots. It has to flip honest agents.

Model. Mixed-effects logistic regression with budget, policy, deployment and budget × policy as fixed effects, plus random intercepts for question and seed/graph. The fit is MAP with Laplace covariance, and inference uses Wald intervals.

Identification boundary: each added sender also adds four deliveries per step, so the budget estimates a bundled sender-plus-exposure treatment. A pure identity-count mechanism is not identified, and the paper does not claim one.

05 · Preregistered tests

H1 to H3 hold in each deployment and pooled

Conditional odds ratios with 95% Wald intervals on a log scale. H1 is the per-sender slope under trust. H2 and H3 compare each policy with trust at the mean positive budget, 7/3.

Deployment estimates are subject-specific. Question heterogeneity is large (random-effect SD 4.15 for Qwen, 3.09 for Mistral), so these odds ratios are larger in magnitude than ratios computed from marginal cell rates. Fitted conditional cell rates reproduce the observed ones within 0.6 points for Qwen and 2.6 points for Mistral. After Holm correction across nine budget-specific comparisons, drop vs. none is significant at b = 2 and b = 4; downgrade vs. none at b = 4 is not (adjusted p = .150).

07 · Takeaway

Keep five layers separate, and let the LLM handle only the last one

The policies do not make the model smarter or detect false math. They change the context a message arrives in. A natural-language claim should never by itself place a message in a trusted relationship channel.

1Enrollment

Provision agent identities and the signed graph. Assumed in the experiment.

2Authentication

Verify the token and its edge binding.

3Relationship authorization

Map a verified sign to an allowed route.

4Routing

Apply none, downgrade or drop before the prompt is built.

5Deliberation (LLM)

Solve the task with only the channels the system exposed.

09 · Scope and next steps

What the evidence supports, and what comes next

Supported
  • Trusting self-declared relationships makes the collective vulnerable to more nominal senders, in both deployments.
  • Deterministic relationship-credential routing reduces that vulnerability before deliberation.
  • Drop is more protective than downgrade against this attack bundle.
Not claimed
  • A pure identity-count mechanism, since senders and exposure are bundled.
  • Benign-traffic utility or a security-utility optimum.
  • A model-family ranking or a calibrated physical threshold.
  • Robustness to stolen keys, compromised enrolled agents or replay.
Next decisive experiments
  • Vary sender labels and total message volume orthogonally.
  • Cross malicious and benign unknown-origin traffic to measure utility.
  • Sample budgets that bracket the 50% crossing, and fit deployment-matched couplings for a quantitative test of the physics model.
  • Add adaptive rhetoric, credential compromise and dynamic graphs.
Why this line of work

The study joins a descriptive model of agent collectives (statistical-mechanics couplings and thresholds) with a security intervention that changes who may exert which kind of influence. The open bridge is quantitative. Can a credential policy be expressed as an effective coupling change that predicts where collective failure begins? Answering that needs the matched-coupling and bracketing-budget experiments above.
